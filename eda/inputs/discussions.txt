Ann Kennedy
Competition Host
Posted a month ago

Great question! The mouse_id is just a mouse identifier (if the field is blank, it's because that information wasn't recorded during the experiment.) The mice in the test set are all different animals from those in the training set- we do this so that it's a true statistically independent test of classifier performance, since different animals can have their own idiosyncratic ways of moving that we don't want to overfit on.

Re conditions- the MABe22 datasets come from an experiment where animals were tracked 24 hours a day for four days, partly to study sleep/wake behavior. "day 1 - 00:40" means 12:40am on day 1 of the recording. Mice are nocturnal and have other circadian rhythms to their behavior, so the timestamp in this data gives you some information about what behavioral states they're likely to be in.

MABe Mouse Behavior Dataset — Exploratory Analysis
1. Dataset Overview
Train videos: 8 789 rows spanning 21 labs; MABe22_keypoints (5 320) and MABe22_movies (2 606) contribute 90 % of the rows.
Behavior-labeled subset: 848 videos (9.7 %) provide non-empty behaviors_labeled; all reside outside the MABe22_* labs.
Test set: Single video (AdaptableSnail, 30 fps, 615 s) with 18 tracked parts and 76 candidate behaviors.
Parquet assets: train_tracking holds 8 789 pose files (one per train row); train_annotation contains 847 label files aligning with the behavior-annotated subset; test_tracking has 1 file.
2. Video Metadata Distributions
Frame rate: Mean 30.4 fps (min 10, max 120.02). Most labs film at 30 fps; notable outliers include JovialSwallow (10 fps) and SparklingTapir (30–120 fps, mean 60.1).
Duration: Mean 134.6 s but highly skewed (median 60 s, max 19 801 s ≈ 5.5 h). MABe22_* clips are fixed 60 s; TranquilPanther spans up to 4 819 s.
Pixel density: Mean 12.4 px/cm with tight clustering at 11.7 (all MABe22_*) and elevated values for SparklingTapir (15–65 px/cm) and InvincibleJellyfish (32 px/cm).
Arena metadata: Shapes are predominantly square (7 961) with smaller rectangular (771), split-rectangular (36), and circular (21) setups. Arena type is mostly “neutral” (8 017) with resident-intruder paradigms (692) and a handful of familiar, CSDS, or blank entries (10).
Tracking method: custom HRnet (7 926) dominates; the remainder uses MARS (528), DeepLabCut (211), or SLEAP (124).
3. Mice & Experimental Context
Group size: 3 mice per video (7 933 rows) is standard; 846 videos track 2 mice, only 10 videos involve 4 mice.
Sex distribution: Strongly male-skewed (25 105 male records vs. 369 female across role slots).
Strains: C57Bl/6J (14 800) and BTBR (9 786) dominate; BALB/c (606) and CD1 families trail.
Colors: Principally black (14 913) and black-and-tan (9 786), with 821 white mice from the CD-1 cohorts.
Conditions & ages: Most logs list “10–20 weeks”; a long tail of circadian experiment stamps (e.g., “day 2- 23:00 (lights off)”) appear in SparklingTapir runs.
4. Body-Part Coverage
Configurations: Only 10 unique body-part sets. The canonical 12-part HRNet layout (ears, nose, tail base/tip, fore/hind paws, body center) covers 7 926 videos.
Counts: Body-part numbers range 4–18 (median 12). Extended headpiece rigs (18 parts) appear in the CalMS21-style recordings; hip points appear in 655 videos.
Per-part frequency: ear_left, ear_right, and tail_base appear in all train rows; body_center is missing from some legacy, 7-part rigs.
5. Behavior Labels
Availability by lab: All CalMS21/CRIM13-style labs (e.g., CalMS21_task1, JovialSwallow, PleasantMeerkat) provide complete label coverage; SparklingTapir labels 54/69 videos; MABe22_* contribute no annotated behaviors.
Segments per video: Behavior counts span 1–76 (median 3, 75th percentile 6). Most videos have sparse annotations; a few multi-action sessions carry dense labels.
Action popularity (agent/target aware):
mouse1,mouse2,sniff (608 clips) and mouse1,mouse2,attack (443) lead pair interactions.
Self behaviors include rear (211), selfgroom (144), and dig (85).
Action popularity (action only): sniff (646), attack (644), sniffgenital (504), approach (354), chase (328), mount (314).
Interaction balance: Pair interactions represent 4 325 labeled behaviors vs. 594 self actions, underscoring the dataset’s focus on social dynamics.
6. Notable Patterns & Data Quality Notes
Massive label imbalance: the bulk of training videos (all MABe22_*) lack annotation files, so supervised training requires either external labels or semi-supervised strategies.
SparklingTapir runs introduce the widest variation in frame rate (30–120 fps), pixel density, and experimental conditions—helpful for generalization but a source of heterogeneity.
A handful of rows contain blank arena_type fields or exotic experimental descriptors; downstream code should defensively handle "" values.
Mouse metadata is male-heavy, so target behaviors featuring females (e.g., maternal or sexual dimorphism) may be underrepresented.
Pose rigs differ (7-, 12-, 18-point layouts). Feature pipelines should expect missing columns when switching labs.

I think so, because the entire Mabe2022 dataset is present in the training set, so it won't be in the test data. Also, the metric evaluation is done on the intersection of the labels that the test has and the behaviors_labeled set by us. Since some labs only want to study a certain action, or label only a certain mouse, even when there are other actions occurring, there is no label for that present in the data (due to the nature of the labs' study). In those cases, we are not penalized for FPs. For example, if we have a 100% accurate sniff detection model, but a lab only annotates "rear", but sniffing occurs in that video and we predict it on the test set, we won't get penalized for it. Same goes for mouse pairs. If only mice 1,2 are being labeled by a lab, and we predict something that actually happens on another pair, let's say 1,3 but that is not present in the ground truth behaviors_labeled, we won't get penalized either. This thread has most of the nuances talked about by the hosts.

This is consistent throughout train and test sets, so a lab annotating for only one action might have 10 other actions happening but lack annotations for those (which are basically false negatives in our training data). Quite the tricky competition.
